Questions About Your Sarnia Request

For commercial duct cleaning in Sarnia, how should I define rooftop and make-up air scope?

List rooftop units and make-up air equipment separately, then ask what inspection, access, and cleaning each includes. If equipment is difficult to reach or has operating restrictions, share that information in advance. A system-by-system scope makes it easier to compare estimates and final invoices; confirm how additional findings or work outside the agreed boundaries would be documented and approved.

What should I review about the ducts before and after in Sarnia?

Ask whether the inspection will include accessible trunk lines and representative branch runs, and whether images will be shared with you. Photographing the same areas afterward makes comparison more useful. A camera can show visible interior conditions, but it does not by itself measure indoor air quality or establish that every part of a system is clean.

For duct sealing repair in Sarnia, how should I test before applying sealant?

Ask what pressure or leakage test is proposed, which parts of the system it covers, and whether results will identify specific leaking joints or punctures. Testing can help target the repair budget, while broad sealing without findings may miss damaged sections. Access, system configuration, and test method affect interpretation, so request the results and the limits of the assessment in writing.

What should I check about the evaporator coil method in Sarnia?

Ask where the evaporator coil is located, whether it can be accessed, and what cleaning method is appropriate for its construction. If deposits are visible, share a photo and ask how fins will be protected during cleaning. Coil condition and access change the work required; a cleaning estimate should distinguish coil treatment from blower service rather than bundling them without explanation.

For duct sanitizing in Sarnia, how should I establish the cleaning sequence?

Ask whether loose debris and residue will be removed before any treatment is considered. Applying a product over dust can leave the underlying material in place, so the proposed sequence matters. Request a description of the areas to be cleaned and treated, and ask how the product is intended to be used on the particular duct surfaces in your system.
